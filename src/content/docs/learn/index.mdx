Welcome to **Photography Field Guide**, a bilingual resource for understanding your camera rather than memorizing recommended settings.

Each core lesson includes a short answer, an intuitive explanation, explicit example settings, misconceptions, a controlled exercise, and sources. English and Simplified Chinese versions follow the same concepts and stable routes.

## Choose your next step

**New to photography?** Follow the stages below, beginning with how a camera works. You do not need to finish every article before taking a photo.

**Ready to make a photo?** Try a [photography mission](../practice/): predict, observe a diagram, then make and review your own pictures. You can enter any mission directly.

**Checking one idea?** Use the [glossary](../glossary/) or the [two calculators](../tools/) and return to the linked explanation when you need context.

## 1 · Understand light and camera settings

1. **[What Is Photography?](./getting-started/what-is-photography/)** — frame a problem in terms of light, time, and intent.
2. **[How a Digital Camera Works](./getting-started/digital-camera-works/)** — follow light into the sensor and processing chain.
3. **[Full Frame, APS-C, and Sensor Formats](./sensor-lens/sensor-formats/)** — compare physical recording areas, not quality scores.
4. **[Equivalent Focal Length](./sensor-lens/equivalent-focal-length/)** — compare field of view without changing the physical lens.
5. **[Aperture and F-Numbers](./exposure/aperture/)** — connect the lens opening with light and acceptable focus.
6. **[Shutter Speed](./exposure/shutter-speed/)** — distinguish subject motion, camera shake, and focus errors.
7. **[ISO Without the Myths](./exposure/iso/)** — separate output brightness from incoming photons.
8. **[Exposure Stops](./exposure/exposure-stops/)** — calculate compensation while preserving the different trade-offs.
9. **[Depth of Field and Background Blur](./sensor-lens/depth-of-field/)** — add distance and viewing conditions to the comparison.
10. **[RAW, JPEG, HEIF, and EXIF](./image-quality/raw-jpeg-exif/)** — separate image encoding from sensor data and metadata.

[The exposure triangle](./exposure/exposure-triangle/) remains a short orientation to the three familiar settings; the individual lessons supply the necessary limits and distinctions.

## 2 · Take control of the camera

Apply the core concepts to the decisions you make while shooting:

1. **[P, A, S, and M Shooting Modes](./exposure/pasm-modes/)** — decide which exposure variables to delegate and which to control directly.
2. **[Exposure Compensation](./exposure/exposure-compensation/)** — adjust an automatic target while watching the parameter that actually changes.
3. **[Autofocus, Focus Modes, and Focus Areas](./image-quality/autofocus/)** — choose when and where to focus without confusing focus with depth of field or motion blur.

## 3 · Choose how color is rendered

Understand the decisions that shape an image after the light is captured:

1. **[White Balance and Color Temperature](./image-quality/white-balance/)** — distinguish the color of light, white-balance compensation, and exposure brightness.
2. **[Film Simulations, Recipes, and RAW Development](./image-quality/film-simulations/)** — compare built-in camera looks, third-party settings, later development, and LUTs without conflating them.

## 4 · Read light and organize the frame

1. **[Understanding Light](./composition/understanding-light/)** — observe direction, shadow edges, and contrast, then try **[Light detective](../practice/light-direction/)**.
2. **[Subject and Background](./composition/subject-and-background/)** — use viewpoint, edges, layers, and negative space, then explain your choices in **[Frame cleanup](../practice/subject-background/)**.

## 5 · Make, select, and review a short photo story

1. **[A City Walk: Tell a Place in Three Photos](./field-practice/neighborhood-story/)** — give environment, subject, and detail distinct information roles, then compare your selection and sequence in **[Three photos, one place](../practice/neighborhood-story/)**.
2. **[Select Photos and Review a Shoot](./field-practice/photo-review/)** — group similar frames, compare editorial fit and technical usability, then choose one next-shoot change in **[Select, review, reshoot](../practice/photo-review/)**.

The first two missions use controlled comparisons; the next two connect three views and turn selection into a new experiment. All are self-reviews rather than aesthetic scores.

## Learn by testing one variable

Read one concept, keep the subject and camera position fixed, and change only one setting. Record what changed beyond brightness. A calculator can compare numeric relationships, but it cannot decide your subject, timing, or desired interpretation.
