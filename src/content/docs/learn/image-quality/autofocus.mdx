Why is the background sharp when the person is not, even though the focus box turned green? A focus confirmation is not a promise that the whole picture will be sharp—or that the camera chose the subject you intended.

**Short answer:** autofocus adjusts the lens for a subject distance. Focus modes determine **how focusing behaves over time**, while focus areas determine **where the camera looks**. Accurate focus, sufficient depth of field, and freedom from motion blur are separate requirements.

## Three different questions

1. **When should the camera adjust or hold focus?** This is the focus-mode decision: AF-S, AF-C, or manual focus on an X-T5.
2. **Which part of the frame should it use?** This is the focus-area decision: a point, zone, or wider tracking area.
3. **What will look acceptably sharp in the final image?** This also depends on depth of field, subject and camera motion, lens performance, and viewing size.

Do not use a larger aperture number as a substitute for choosing the right subject. More depth of field can increase the acceptable-focus range, but it cannot repair every focus error.

## Focus behavior over time

| X-T5 focus mode | Normal half-press behavior                                               | A useful starting situation                          |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **S (AF-S)**    | Single AF locks focus while the shutter is held halfway                  | Stationary or nearly stationary subjects             |
| **C (AF-C)**    | Continuous AF adjusts for changes in subject distance while held halfway | Subjects moving toward or away from the camera       |
| **M (Manual)**  | You focus using the lens focus ring                                      | Deliberate manual control or difficult AF conditions |

Names and controls vary by manufacturer. Button customization, lens switches, and options such as Pre-AF can alter the detailed behavior, so read the camera display and manual rather than relying only on the letter.

## Focus area: where the camera should look

- **Single point:** you select a small area for precise placement. It is useful when the intended subject is specific, but a point placed on the background can focus the background.
- **Zone:** a group of points gives the subject more room within a selected region. It can help with predictable motion, but another high-contrast object inside the zone may compete.
- **Wide or tracking:** the available behavior depends on whether AF-S or AF-C is active. On the X-T5, AF-S offers Wide and AF-C offers Tracking; these are not interchangeable labels for one universal algorithm.
- **Face, eye, or subject detection:** a separate assistance option on supported cameras. Detection and focus-area configuration still need inspection; a detected subject is not guaranteed to remain sharply focused.

## Worked examples

**Static architecture:** AF-S with a carefully placed single point is a clear starting choice. Check that the point covers the intended surface rather than a distant object behind glass.

**A person walking toward you:** AF-C with an appropriate zone or tracking area gives the camera an opportunity to update focus. You still need a shutter speed that controls subject motion and an active area that keeps the person included.

**Low contrast or obstructions:** the X-T5 manual lists low-contrast subjects, shiny surfaces, subjects behind reflective material, and competing high-contrast objects as difficult cases. Change the area or viewpoint, find a more usable target at the same distance, or use manual focus with magnification when appropriate.

## A sharpness diagnosis is not just an AF diagnosis

- **Intended subject soft, another distance sharp:** inspect focus placement and depth of field.
- **Everything streaked in one direction:** consider camera movement or subject motion, not just missed focus.
- **Near and far details both soft:** inspect focus, motion, lens settings, and output viewing conditions.
- **Focus hunts or switches to another object:** reconsider contrast, focus-area size, obstructions, and the selected focus behavior.

Autofocus cannot freeze a moving person, and a fast shutter cannot move the focus distance onto the person.

## X-T5 control sequence

1. Set the focus-mode selector to **S**, **C**, or **M**.
2. For autofocus, choose **AF/MF SETTING → AF MODE**.
3. Select the focus area with the focus stick and adjust its size as described in the manual.
4. Half-press while watching the selected area and focus indicator, then review important frames at useful magnification.

The official manual notes that enabling **Pre-AF** makes the camera adjust focus continuously in S and C even without a shutter half-press. That is why a generic “AF-S always waits” rule is incomplete.

## Practice: isolate time and area

First, use a stationary high-contrast object with fixed exposure and framing. Compare AF-S and AF-C using the same single point; record whether the results differ in this controlled situation.

Next, ask a person to walk toward you at a safe, repeatable pace. Use AF-C, keep the active area on the person, and make a short sequence. Inspect the intended face rather than judging only the thumbnail. Repeat with an unsuitable point aimed at the background to demonstrate why area selection matters.

Finally, try a low-contrast stationary object. If autofocus struggles, change the target or use manual-focus assistance. Record the limitation instead of treating every missed frame as user failure.
