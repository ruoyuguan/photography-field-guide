“Full frame” and “APS-C” describe physical recording formats. They are not letter grades, and APS-C is not one universal sensor dimension.

**Short answer:** with the same lens and camera position, a smaller sensor records a smaller part of the projected image and therefore a narrower field of view. It does not change the lens's focal length or f-number.

## Start with real dimensions

| Example               | Dimensions       | Diagonal crop factor relative to 36 × 24 mm |
| --------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Full-frame reference  | 36 × 24 mm       | 1×                                          |
| Nikon DX example      | About 24 × 16 mm | About 1.5×                                  |
| Fujifilm X-T5 example | 23.5 × 15.7 mm   | About 1.53×                                 |

Crop factor here compares diagonals for formats with matching aspect ratios. The X-T5 area is about 43% of the full-frame reference—not exactly half. “APS-C” alone is not enough for an exact calculation.

## Four comparisons to keep separate

1. **Field of view:** a smaller recording area crops the view when position and lens stay fixed.
2. **Total light:** at the same f-number and exposure time, the smaller area collects less total light in the simplified same-scene comparison. That is different from light per unit area.
3. **Perspective:** it depends on camera position. Moving back to restore framing changes perspective because you moved.
4. **Sensor performance:** pixel design, readout, processing, generation, and output conditions matter. Format alone does not rank every camera's noise or image quality.

## Worked example settings

Use **50 mm, F4, 1/125 s, ISO 400** on both formats without moving. The APS-C frame has a tighter view. Its actual lens remains 50 mm and its aperture remains F4. **Do not multiply ordinary exposure settings by crop factor.** Metering implementations can vary, but there is no universal exposure-format conversion.

If you instead choose a wider lens or move back to match framing, the comparison changes. Depth of field and total-light discussions now require those conditions to be explicit.

## Common misconceptions

- **APS-C means exactly half full-frame area.** Real dimensions vary; the X-T5 example is about 43% by area.
- **A smaller sensor magnifies the lens.** It records a smaller region; focal length remains unchanged.
- **Full frame is always better.** Formats involve size, lenses, cost, output, and photographic intent; “better” needs a use case.

## Practice: crop without moving

Photograph a static scene using one lens, fixed example settings, and a locked camera position. Crop a copy in software to the X-T5-to-full-frame linear proportion, or compare two cameras if available. Keep output display size consistent and note what changes in framing. Do not move closer for the first comparison.
