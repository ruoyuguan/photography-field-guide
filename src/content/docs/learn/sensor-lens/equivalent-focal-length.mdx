**Equivalent focal length** is a way to describe field of view using full frame as a common reference. It answers a practical question:

> Which lens on a full-frame camera would give a similar field of view from the same camera position?

For formats with the same aspect ratio, the comparison is:

**Full-frame-equivalent focal length = actual focal length × crop factor**

## Example

A 50 mm lens used on an APS-C camera with a 1.5× crop factor has a field of view similar to:

**50 mm × 1.5 = 75 mm on full frame**

The lens has not become a 75 mm lens. Its optical design and marked focal length remain 50 mm. “75 mm equivalent” describes only the resulting field of view.

A real manufacturer example is the XF16–50mmF2.8–4.8: its physical range is 16–50 mm, and Fujifilm lists a 24–76 mm full-frame-equivalent range.

## Common crop factors

- Nikon and Fujifilm APS-C: often about **1.5×**
- Other APS-C models and sensor formats can differ. Check the exact sensor specification when precision matters.

## Do not mix up these ideas

- **Focal length:** a physical property of the lens.
- **Field of view:** how much of the scene the camera records; it depends on focal length, sensor size, and other geometric factors.
- **Perspective:** the relative size and position of objects; it depends on camera position.
- **Aperture f-number:** it does not change when you move the lens to another sensor format.

Equivalence is a comparison tool, not a conversion performed inside the lens.
