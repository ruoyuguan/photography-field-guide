Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO are often grouped as the **exposure triangle**. They all influence image brightness, but they do not all change the amount of light reaching the sensor.

## Aperture

Aperture is the opening through which light passes. The f-number is approximately the lens focal length divided by the entrance-pupil diameter.

- A **lower f-number**, such as f/1.8, usually admits more light per unit time than f/5.6.
- Aperture also influences **depth of field** and background blur, together with focal length, focus distance, sensor size, and background distance.

## Shutter speed

Shutter speed controls how long the sensor collects light during the exposure.

- A **slower shutter speed** admits more light but can increase motion blur.
- A **faster shutter speed** admits less light but can freeze movement more effectively.

## ISO

ISO changes how strongly the captured signal is amplified or processed into the final image.

- Higher ISO can make the image brighter.
- Higher ISO does **not** allow more light through the lens.
- At a fixed aperture and shutter speed, raising ISO does not increase the number of photons that reached the sensor.

## A warning about “equivalent aperture”

Crop factor must not be applied directly to your exposure settings. Moving a 50 mm f/1.8 lens to an APS-C camera does not change its marked focal length or f-number, and you do not need to multiply shutter speed or ISO by the crop factor to obtain a normal exposure.

Other equivalence discussions—such as depth of field, total light, or noise—require their own conditions and should be explained separately from exposure brightness.

**Try it yourself:** compare two settings with the [exposure stops calculator](../../../tools/exposure-stops/). Start by changing only ISO and watch the light-exposure result remain unchanged.
